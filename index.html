Made

One hide. Cut by hand.

The pattern is cut from a single hide, so the grain runs continuous from the front of the bag to the back. It is slower, it wastes more leather, and it is the only way the thing looks like one object instead of several.

  1. 01

    The cut

    One hide per bag. The pattern is laid by eye, around the marks the animal already had, so no two bodies fall the same way.

  2. 02

    The edge

    Every edge is painted and burnished by hand until it is closed and hard. This is the part that fails first on a cheap bag, and the part nobody photographs.

  3. 03

    The hardware

    Gold-tone, set flush to the body. Nothing stands proud of the leather, so there is nothing to catch and nothing to wear through.

Tapwa — of God

Named for its maker.

Tapwa is a Bwatiye name from Adamawa, in the north-east of Nigeria. It means of God. It was carried to Canada by the woman whose work this is, and it is the name she puts on the bags because it is the one she was given.

The label is stamped, not printed. It is the only word on the bag.

TAP-wah · Bwatiye, Adamawa State, Nigeria

A run of fifty

Fifty bags, then the pattern is retired.

The Crescent is made in a run of fifty. There is no restock and no second colourway — when the fifty are gone the pattern goes in a drawer, and the next one is a different shape.

If you want one, write. Tell me your name and where you are, and I will tell you what is left and what it costs.
